# How Staff+ Can Develop Strategic Thinking

_Summary of Shweta Saraf's article (Netflix)_

## Central Idea

Strategic thinking is the ability to make decisions considering not only current problems but also their long-term impacts on the organization. For Staff+ engineers, this skill complements traditional technical thinking and expands their influence beyond execution.

---

# Why Strategic Thinking Matters

Engineers typically master:

- Critical thinking (logical analysis)
- Creative thinking (innovation)

But strategic thinking adds:

- Long-term vision
- Adaptability
- Alignment with organizational objectives
- Better decision-making under uncertainty

Examples like Kodak and Blockbuster show how lack of strategic vision can lead to failure even in leading companies.

---

# The Good Strategy Framework (Richard Rumelt)

An effective strategy has three components:

## 1. Diagnosis

Understanding:

- What is the problem
- Why it matters
- Who is impacted

## 2. Guiding Policy

Define:

- Principles
- Values
- Decision guidelines

Should remain relatively stable over time.

## 3. Coherent Action

Transform strategy into execution through:

- Roadmaps
- Priorities
- Concrete initiatives

Without execution, strategy is just an idea.

---

# Three Pitfalls of Bad Strategies

## 1. Focusing on "How" Before "Why"

Error:

- Discussing solutions before understanding the problem.

Consequence:

- Checklists without purpose.
- Demotivating execution.

---

## 2. Losing Context During Execution

Error:

- Copying market solutions without adapting them to company reality.

Consequence:

- Strategies that look good in theory but don't deliver results.

---

## 3. Being Overly Tactical

Error:

- Solving only immediate problems.

Consequence:

- Lack of sustainability.
- Constant rework.

---

# Practical Framework for Strategy Building

## Step 1 — Understand Your Customers

Map all stakeholders:

- End users
- Product teams
- Engineering
- Platform
- Business

---

## Step 2 — Discover Real Pain Points

Conduct:

- Interviews
- Stakeholder conversations
- Direct observation

Objective:

Identify fundamental problems, not just symptoms.

---

## Step 3 — Generate Insights

The Staff+ role is to:

- Synthesize information
- Identify patterns
- Transform data into strategic direction

---

## Step 4 — Look Outward

Analyze:

- Industry trends
- Market solutions
- Build vs Buy

Question:

> Is it worth building or buying?

---

## Step 5 — Define Strategic Principles

Create 2-3 pillars to sustain decisions for 12-18 months.

Examples:

- Developer Experience
- Reliability
- Scalability
- Operational Efficiency

---

## Step 6 — Define Metrics

The strategy should have:

### Leading Indicators

Early signals of success.

Ex.:

- Deployment time
- Platform adoption

### Lagging Indicators

Final results.

Ex.:

- Revenue
- Availability
- Retention

---

# Balancing Risk and Innovation

Before proposing changes, understand:

## What does the company value?

Some prioritize:

- Execution
- Revenue
- Profit

Others prioritize:

- Data
- Experimentation
- Innovation

Others value:

- Relationships
- Influence
- Narrative

---

# Tools for Risk Reduction

## Rapid Prototyping

Better:

- 2 days building a prototype

Than:

- 2 months discussing an idea.

---

## Hackathons and Ideathons

Allow:

- Controlled experimentation
- Rapid learning
- Opportunity discovery

---

# Framework of Knowns and Unknowns

## Known Knowns

We know that we know.

Ex.:

- Team competencies
- Mastered systems

---

## Known Unknowns

We know risks exist.

Ex.:

- Hypotheses not yet validated

Tools:

- Prototypes
- Pre-mortems
- Simulations

---

## Unknown Knowns

Unconscious bias.

We know something but don't realize how it influences our decisions.

Solution:

- Peer feedback
- External review

---

## Unknown Unknowns

Unpredictable surprises.

Ex.:

- Market changes
- Unprecedented failures

Strategy:

- Continuous innovation investment
- Chaos Engineering
- Experimentation

# Lessons from Experienced Staff+

## Distinguished Engineer

Learnings:

- Prioritize ruthlessly.
- Don't try to decide everything.
- Expand your knowledge to:
  - Legal
  - Compliance
  - Finance
- Trust is built over time.

---

## Principal Engineer

Main learning:

> Relationships are as important as technical excellence.

Without trust, strategic influence is limited.

---

## SRE Staff+

Learnings:

- Multiply your impact through others.
- Influence scales better than individual execution.
- Alternating between IC and management builds broader vision.

---

## Depth Engineer

Learnings:

- Understand what the organization truly values.
- Treat engineering time as currency.
- Invest heavily in written communication.

---

# Cultural Influence

Staff+ influences not just systems.

Also influences:

- Culture
- Behaviors
- Processes
- People

Mechanisms:

- Lead by example
- Mentor other engineers
- Participate in organizational decisions

---

# Leadership's Role

Leaders should:

- Share business context
- Give visibility to Staff+
- Include Staff+ in executive discussions
- Protect time for strategic work
- "Stress test" proposed strategies

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Strategy = Diagnosis + Principles + Execution.
2. Always start with "why".
3. Context matters more than frameworks.
4. Prototyping is better than debating indefinitely.
5. Influence is as important as technical knowledge.
6. Relationships amplify impact.
7. Staff+ should connect technology and business.
8. Engineering time is a strategic resource.
9. Metrics are essential to validate bets.
10. Strategic thinking is a skill that develops deliberately.
